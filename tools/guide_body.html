The short answer

  • Do it yourself with a website builder: about $17–$49 a month, plus 10–20 hours of your time to set it up.
  • Hire a freelance designer: usually $500–$5,000 up front, and updates cost extra.
  • Hire an agency for a custom site: often $5,000 and up.
  • Local marketing subscriptions: often $99–$449 a month, usually with a contract.
  • Done-for-you packages for small shops, like ours: $299–$999 for the first year, then a small yearly fee.

For most local shops (a restaurant, a salon, a repair shop), a simple, fast, mobile site is all you need. Spending more rarely brings more customers.

Option 1: Build it yourself

Website builders like Squarespace and Wix give you templates, hosting and a free domain for the first year. Squarespace plans start at $19 a month billed yearly, and Wix's paid plans start at about $17 a month. Plans with online selling or booking cost more.

Good fit if you enjoy this kind of work and have the time. Watch out for the domain renewing at full price in year two, business email billed separately for each person, and the hours it takes to write, photograph and lay out the site. Many owners start a site and never finish it.

Option 2: Hire a freelancer

A freelance designer usually charges $500 to $5,000 depending on the number of pages and features. You get a site made for you, but hosting, domain, email and future changes are often separate.

Good fit if you need something custom and have a clear idea of it. Watch out for freelancers who disappear after launch, sites built on accounts in their name, and hourly fees every time your hours change.

Option 3: Hire an agency

Agencies build custom sites with strategy, copywriting and design teams. Expect $5,000 and up, often much more. For a single-location shop, this is usually more than you need.

Option 4: A monthly marketing subscription

Some companies build your site for little or nothing up front and then charge $99 to $449 a month, often with a 12-month contract. Some include ads or "SEO" services.

Watch out for who owns the domain and the site. If you cancel and they keep both, you start over with a new address and lose the reviews and links pointing to the old one.

The hidden costs to ask about

  • Renewal prices. A "free domain" or low first-year price often doubles in year two.
  • Email per person. Business email is usually billed per address, every month.
  • Who owns the .com. It should be registered in your name, with you able to log in.
  • Contracts and cancellation fees. Know how to leave before you sign up.
  • Paid add-ons. Booking tools, online ordering and apps can each add a monthly fee.
  • Your time. A "cheap" site that takes 20 hours of your evenings isn't cheap.

What a small business site actually needs

For a local shop, a site that does these things well beats a fancy one that doesn't:

  1. Looks right and loads fast on a phone. Most of your visitors are on one.
  2. Shows your hours, address and a tap-to-call button at the top.
  3. Lists what you sell or do, with prices where you can.
  4. Has real photos of your food, work or shop.
  5. Lives on your own .com, registered in your name, with a secure https connection.
  6. Matches your Google Business Profile: same name, hours, phone and address.
  7. Is easy to update when something changes.

Five questions to ask before you pay anyone

  1. Is the domain registered in my name, and can I log in to it?
  2. What will I pay in year two, all in: hosting, domain, email and updates?
  3. How do I ask for changes, and how fast do they happen?
  4. If I leave, do I keep the domain and the site files?
  5. Can I see the site before I pay?
